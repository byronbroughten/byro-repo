# Person and register: instances

Disclosed from [`docs/writing-style.md`](../writing-style.md), "Person and register". The rules are there, one line each; this file holds the quoted instances and their sources. "Seen in" counts the reading batches, out of 6, that reported the habit ([sources](./sources.md)). "In N of 5 posts" counts the blog posts.

## "You", "we" and "let's"

- "DealLab emphasizes detailed cost estimation by giving users the option to break down any category" — DealLab - Brand Presentation
- "Now that we have covered the relevant considerations of Wild Dog Coffee Company, let's talk about supply chains and logistics." — Assessment 3 (Wild Dog Coffee supply chain)
- "To sidestep such underestimation and regret, let's consider all the costs of owning a home" — blog: Costs of Owning a Home
- "we think it's worth asking: is buying a house a good investment" — blog: Is Buying a House a Good Investment?
- "Users" was seen in 2 of 6 batches; "we" and "let's" had no count, and the samples limited them to scripts. "You", "we" and "let's" are in 5 of 5 posts, and the posts outrank the samples ([sources](./sources.md)).
- Academic work writes in the impersonal third person instead ([`academic-writing-style.md`](../academic-writing-style.md)).

## Contractions

- "Paylocity's customer support team isn't particularly special, so other companies could imitate it." — BB - Stratigic Analysis and Recommendations for Paylocity
- "Owning a home isn't cheap." — blog: Home Cost Comparison
- Seen in 3 of 6. In 5 of 5 posts.

## Plain words, unless the Latinate one is more precise

- "Users can tailor the app to their needs" — DealLab - Brand Presentation
- Plain over Latinate: "show", not "corroborate"; "many", not "manifold".
- "Settle for a less expensive property." — blog: Costs of Owning a Home
- Seen in 3 of 6. The reports lean Latinate ("corroborate" about 10 times, "salient", "attenuates"); the deck does not, and the deck wins. In 5 of 5 posts.
- The developer ruled in review that plain words are the default, but a Latinate word stays when it says the intended meaning more precisely.
- The developer kept "corroborates" in a sworn letter after the agent flagged it twice, and ruled (2026-10-05) that it is an excellent, precise word in formal pieces, such as a sworn letter or academic work, but not in blog posts for a general audience. — session 517e5e68, 2026-10-05

## Everyday idioms, slightly twisted

- "the total ongoing home costs would drop by a whopping $1,104 per month" — blog: Home Cost Comparison
- "A 20% often provides good bang-for-buck in this regard" — blog: Rent vs Buy
- "The average homeowner who pays HOA fees shells out around $250 per month" — blog: Costs of Owning a Home
- In 5 of 5 posts. "A whopping" is in 3 of them.
- The developer ruled in review (2026-10-02) that they now prefer idioms slightly twisted, such as "blow out of the pond".
