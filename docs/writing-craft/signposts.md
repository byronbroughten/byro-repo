# Signposts: examples

Disclosed from [`docs/writing-craft.md`](../writing-craft.md), "Signposts". The rules are there, one line each; this file holds each rule's before-and-after and where in Pinker's _The Sense of Style_ it comes from. An instance marked _from Pinker; not yet seen in your writing_ is invented, and a real one replaces it when it turns up.

## The reader moves, not the text

- Before: "The previous section showed that the budget depends on the new HELOC." After: "As we saw, the budget depends on the new HELOC." — invented; Pinker, Ch. 2, PDF pp. 46–48. _From Pinker; not yet seen in your writing._
- The academic sheet's purpose statement ("This report is to inform…") overrides it in academic work. The Voice sheet's "This section identifies…" and "No preview list" lines overlap; #321's audit decides whether they move.

## A reference ahead says so

- Agent: "in a fixed order, which the WBS does not show" Developer: "in a fixed order, which the upcoming WBS only incidentally shows" — personal#16 Snapshot, 2026-10-26
- The developer: a reader assumes by default that a named section is one already read, so a forward reference has to say it points ahead.

## A same-named section

- Agent: "The charter's performance, cost, and time constraints, set out under Constraints, bound the scope" Developer: "set out under the Constraints earlier, are what binds the project's scope" — personal#16 Snapshot, 2026-10-26
- The developer: the sentence sat in a section titled "Constraint(s):", so a bare "Constraints" could read as the section the reader was in.
