# Words and terms: examples

Disclosed from [`docs/writing-craft.md`](../writing-craft.md), "Words". The rules are there, one line each; this file holds each rule's before-and-after and where in Pinker's _The Sense of Style_ it comes from. An instance marked _from Pinker; not yet seen in your writing_ is invented, and a real one replaces it when it turns up.

## Name the thing, not its category

- Before: "The project faces affordability issues at the current rate level." After: "At 7.5%, rent may not cover the mortgage." — invented; Pinker, Ch. 2, PDF pp. 56–57; Ch. 3, PDF pp. 79–80. _From Pinker; not yet seen in your writing._
- The Voice sheet's "cutting any wrapper phrase" overlaps; #321's audit decides whether it moves.

## What it is, not the part it plays

- Before: "The subject property's mechanical systems may require capital expenditure." After: "The duplex's 1950s boiler may need replacing." — invented; Pinker, Ch. 3, PDF pp. 77–80. _From Pinker; not yet seen in your writing._

## Coined names in plain words

- Before: "Under Option B, the BH covers any overrun." After: "If repairs overrun, the backup HELOC covers the rest." — invented; Pinker, Ch. 3, PDF pp. 72–73. _From Pinker; not yet seen in your writing._
- The Voice sheet's acronym rule still governs an established acronym (CapEx, HELOC); this rule covers names invented for the piece. "Refer back by a short name" keeps "Table 2" by the exception.

## Gloss a term of the trade

- Agent and developer both: "cash-on-cash return, i.e., the property's annual net cash flow, after the mortgage payment and operating costs, divided by the cash invested to acquire it" — Assessment 1 Next Draft (writing#4), a gloss the draft got right.
- Before: "The property must clear a 1.25 DSCR." After: "The property must clear a 1.25 DSCR, i.e., its rent must cover the mortgage payment 1.25 times." — invented; Pinker, Ch. 3, PDF pp. 71–72, 76–77. _From Pinker; not yet seen in your writing._
- The Voice sheet's "i.e.," rule decides the gloss's form; this rule decides when one is needed.

## Everyday words when they're as exact

- Before: "Gross scheduled income must exceed debt service by $750 a month." After: "Rent must exceed the mortgage payment by $750 a month." — invented; Pinker, Ch. 3, PDF p. 71. _From Pinker; not yet seen in your writing._
- The Voice sheet's "Prefer plain words to Latinate ones" overlaps; #321's audit decides whether it moves.

## Quotation marks, not scare quotes

- Before: "Jose is a 'go-to' contractor for turnovers." After: "Jose is our go-to contractor for turnovers." — invented; Pinker, Ch. 2, PDF pp. 50–51. _From Pinker; not yet seen in your writing._
- It sits with the Voice sheet's idiom rule: use the idiom plainly, or don't use it.

## No clashing idioms

- Before: "The HELOC is our safety net, so we can hit the ground running." After: "The HELOC is our safety net if repairs run over." — invented; Pinker, Ch. 2, PDF pp. 53–54. _From Pinker; not yet seen in your writing._
- It guards the Voice sheet's idiom habit: an idiom keeps its picture, so two pictures can collide.
