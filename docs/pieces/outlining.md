# Outlining a Piece

How to write a spec's Outline.

## What an Outline holds

One entry per Section, in reading order:

- **The heading**, as it will read in the Deliverable.
- **The claim**: what the Section says, in one line.
- **The facts it sets out**, in full, drawn from the spec's Facts field. A fact a later Section relies on is set out once, in the first Section that needs it, and that Section coins any short name for it.

Nothing more: no wording, no transitions, no style notes. Those belong to the drafter and the Style field.

## Ordering the Sections

Order the Sections so each next one is the turn the reader expects: general to specific, larger to smaller, earlier to later, or the order things happened. A Piece ordered that way needs few signposts. (Pinker, _The Sense of Style_, Ch. 2, PDF p. 47.)

## When a template fixes the Sections

An assignment's template, named in the Form field, fixes the headings and their order. The Outline keeps them as given and fills each with its claim and facts; it adds, drops or reorders a Section only with the developer's yes.

## When Research revises it

Research revises the Outline only when its verified list changes the facts: a fact dropped, corrected or added. It then edits the affected entries and says which in its comment. A source that only confirms a fact leaves the Outline as it is.
