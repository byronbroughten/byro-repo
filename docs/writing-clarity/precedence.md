# Precedence: examples

Disclosed from [`docs/writing-clarity.md`](../writing-clarity.md), "Precedence". The rule is there, one line; this file holds why it is drawn where it is and the cases it has decided.

## Why a misread is the line

A Voice habit is how the developer sounds, and a reader who parses it the first time loses nothing by it, so the habit stays even where a guide would write the sentence differently. A habit that makes the reader misread or reread a sentence costs the reader the text itself, which is what Clarity exists to protect, so there the Clarity rule wins. The developer set the line in #309, when the style sheet split into Voice and Clarity.

## Arithmetic keeps its steps

- Strunk and White's advice to combine a run of short sentences that build one point meets the developer's habit of showing arithmetic one step per sentence, then the total ([`writing-voice.md`](../writing-voice.md), "Numbers and evidence"). The steps are a matter of taste: a reader follows each one on the first pass, so the habit stays, and the Voice sheet's combining rule carries the exception "arithmetic keeps its steps".
