# Borrowing: reasoning and examples

Disclosed from [`docs/writing-voice.md`](../writing-voice.md), "Numbers and evidence": the paraphrase and quote rules. This file holds why the floor is four words, what it catches, and where the numbers came from.

## Restate a borrowed fact

- Four words is the strict end of published guidance. Old Dominion, Iowa and UCL ask for quotation marks around more than three, or three or more, consecutive words; Trent says two or three, and also counts the source's sentence structure with swapped words; Indiana's plagiarism test uses seven. No standard sets a single number.
- Turnitin's "exclude small matches" defaults to 8 words, which is also its floor, so an instructor sees runs of 8 or more by default. Four leaves margin below that.
- The floor applies only to passages read in the course of the work, so an agent never searches a whole Reference for chance matches.
- Runs of common function words ("at the end of the") are noise and never count; proper names and fixed terms ("Housing Choice Voucher program", "cash-on-cash return") can't be reworded and don't count.
- What a four-word floor catches (illustrative, from writing#2's domain):

| Draft | Passage read | Run | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| "a risk is an uncertain event or condition that could affect the schedule" | PMBOK: "an uncertain event or condition that, if it occurs, has a positive or negative effect…" | 6 | Copied definition |
| "may not exceed ten financed properties" | Fannie Mae: "…may not exceed ten financed properties…" | 5 | The rule's wording, not the writer's |
| "the landlord must provide written notice" | Saint Paul: "the landlord must provide the tenant…" | 4 | Borderline; judged by eye |
| "at the end of the lease term" | any | 5 | Function words; ignored |

- Rubric wording is the likeliest Supporting Material to leak: a drafter aiming at a criterion echoes its phrasing in topic sentences, and other students' papers quoting the same rubric may sit in Turnitin's database.

Sources: [Old Dominion](https://odu.edu/sites/default/files/documents/about-plagiarism2.pdf), [Iowa](https://engineering.uiowa.edu/sites/engineering.uiowa.edu/files/2021-03/hctc_guide_to_avoiding_plagiarism.pdf), [UCL](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/ioe-writing-centre/reference-effectively-avoid-plagiarism/paraphrasing), [Trent](https://trentu.ca/history/node/143), [Indiana](https://plagiarism.indiana.edu/hints.html), [Turnitin](https://guides.turnitin.com/hc/en-us/articles/23539146689549). ## Quote only when notably better

- Outside academic work a quote earns its place only when its wording beats any paraphrase. Academic work quotes nothing ([`academic-writing-style.md`](../academic-writing-style.md)): the grader is judging the writer's analysis, and a quote adds none.
